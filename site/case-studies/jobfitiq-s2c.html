RENDERED PAGE WALKTHROUGH

Can a candidate understand the product and inspect proof before joining the waitlist?

https://jobfitiq.com/ · Rendered DOM; desktop captures at 1280 × 720 and a mobile capture at 390 × 844 · Callouts are review observations, not measured gaze.

First viewport · orient and choose a path

12
  1. strength

    1. Purpose and proof route are visible

    Observed: The headline and summary explain a job-specific candidate evidence brief, and a link to Paul's infographic résumé appears in the first viewport.

    Likely task effect: A proof-seeking visitor has a direct route to a finished example. This effect has not been tested with readers.

    Check: Ask new visitors where they would click to inspect a finished résumé.

  2. review

    2. Competing first actions

    Observed: The filled waitlist button carries more visual weight than the adjacent résumé-example link.

    Likely task effect: A visitor evaluating the product may notice sign-up before inspecting the evidence. This is a hypothesis.

    Suggestion: Consider giving the finished example equal emphasis for visitors who are not ready to sign up.

    Check: Record first clicks for the task of finding proof before joining.

Transformation studio · understand the starting state

12
  1. strength

    1. Inputs are grouped as steps

    Observed: The studio places résumé upload and job description in two numbered groups with short supporting labels.

    Likely task effect: This may make the required inputs easier to find and compare. Reader success is unmeasured.

    Check: Ask visitors to identify the two inputs required for their own brief.

  2. review

    2. Sample state needs an explicit label

    Observed: The upload area says 'Paul Woll Resume.docx' is ready to analyze, and the job description already contains a role, without a prominent shared 'sample data' label.

    Likely task effect: New visitors may be unsure whether they are seeing a demo or their own current inputs. This is a hypothesis.

    Suggestion: Label the preloaded résumé and role as an example state and clarify how to replace them.

    Check: Ask first-time visitors whose data is currently in the studio and what happens when they start.

Live sample · inspect claims in context

12
  1. review

    1. Numeric signals need visible definitions

    Observed: The preview shows '92 role fit' and '95 research → prod' without a visible scale or explanation in this view.

    Likely task effect: Readers may assign different meanings to those numbers. This remains untested.

    Suggestion: Add a concise scale or method cue beside each score, with a route to the supporting evidence.

    Check: Ask readers what 92 and 95 mean, then compare answers with the intended definitions.

  2. strength

    2. Requirements connect to evidence

    Observed: The preview groups Production ML, Python/data pipelines, and Cloud/DevOps under a 'Requirement → Evidence' heading.

    Likely task effect: The structure may support targeted comparison before a close read. This effect is not measured.

    Check: Ask a recruiter to locate the strongest and weakest supported requirement.

Mobile first viewport · check the proof route

12
  1. strength

    1. The example survives the narrow layout

    Observed: At 390 × 844, both the waitlist button and Paul's infographic résumé link remain in the first viewport.

    Likely task effect: A visitor can still choose a proof route without opening the menu. This effect is untested.

    Check: Ask mobile visitors to locate a completed example before signing up.

  2. review

    2. The headline takes substantial vertical space

    Observed: The large headline wraps across five lines in the 390-pixel view.

    Likely task effect: It may delay the detailed explanation for visitors who start below the header. This is a hypothesis.

    Suggestion: Compare this version with a slightly shorter or smaller mobile heading while preserving the meaning.

    Check: Observe whether new mobile visitors can explain the product and find the example after a brief first view.
