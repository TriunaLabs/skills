# S2C pattern map

NN/g's [report overview](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/how-people-read-online-eyetracking-evidence/) names F, layer-cake, spotted, commitment, bypassing, zigzag, and lawn-mower behavior among others. Its [text-pattern article](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-scanning-patterns-eyetracking/) calls F, spotted, layer-cake, and commitment the four main patterns for *textual information*. Lawn-mower scanning is discussed particularly for comparison tables. A reader may appraise a page, switch patterns, backtrack, and focus on only part of a table. Never label a pattern as a property of a page without observing readers.

| Pattern or behavior | When it may appear | Design response | Check for failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **F-pattern** | Long, weakly structured prose; in left-to-right reading, attention often favors the top and beginnings of lines. Right-to-left reading can mirror it. | Front-load meaningful words in title, lead, headings, and paragraph openings; add genuine section structure and lists where appropriate. | Does a scan of opening words reveal the topic without hiding qualifications? Avoid claiming a fixed share of content is ignored. |
| **Layer-cake** | Readers scan visually distinct headings and skip body text until a heading matches their task. | Write headings that say what the section answers; keep the following text faithful to the heading. Give sections enough visual separation to be found. | Can a heading-only pass locate the right section, and does close reading deliver the promised answer? |
| **Spotted** | Readers seek task-relevant words, links, numbers, acronyms, bullets, or visually differentiated text across the page. | Make a few useful anchors explicit: descriptive link labels, directly labeled metrics, informative list starts, and selective emphasis. | Are the anchors meaningful out of context? Does over-highlighting create competition or bury caveats? |
| **Lawn mower** | Readers compare adjacent cells across rows, often moving across one row and back across the next. Most documented on comparison tables. | Keep column identity available deep in the table; make cells interpretable; explain jargon; group repeated or empty values; allow focused comparison when many columns exist. | Can a reader compare two options without repeatedly returning to a distant header? Are cells meaningful when encountered before the row label? |
| **Commitment reading** | A motivated reader studies most of the text, often for consequential or personally important tasks. This is reading, not a scanning pattern. | Preserve methods, definitions, sources, exceptions, and coherent paragraphs. Use scan aids to get the reader into the right detail. | Can the reader explain the answer accurately after close reading? |
| **Bypassing** | Repeated openings in lists may be skipped because they carry no new information. | Put discriminating words early; remove repeated boilerplate from each item when a shared heading can carry it. | Do item starts distinguish choices? Do not vary grammar merely for novelty. |
| **Zigzag layouts** | Alternating image and text sides can send attention across a page; decorative images can also impede efficient scanning. | Use a stable text alignment for repeated modules unless an actual relationship benefits from alternation. If sequence matters, number and label it explicitly. | Can a reader follow the sequence at mobile width and when images are skipped? |

### S2C design moves

- **F-pattern shield:** improve the information carried by the top and line starts, then provide headings and meaningful grouping. It cannot prevent an F-shaped scanpath.
- **Layer-cake grid:** let headings form a usable outline. Do not make every heading a forced “takeaway” if the section asks a question or describes a method.
- **Spotted intercept:** place task-relevant anchors where they help retrieval. High contrast is useful only when text, hierarchy, and surrounding context remain legible.
- **Lawn-mower track:** support comparison with stable labels, cells, and row grouping. Decorative directional rails or arrows are not required.

These moves can coexist on one page. Choose by the reader's task, not by an assumed universal sequence of fixations. ## Visual hierarchy without false precision

- **Component saliency:** size, contrast, position, and component type can affect predicted free-viewing attention. In S2C, assign visual weight to the most consequential answer or comparison, then confirm that the required context remains close. Do not assume a face, logo, or hero always receives the first fixation.
- **Alignment and whitespace:** use stable alignment to make repeated items comparable and whitespace to show grouping. A deliberate break may signal a new section, but “zero-baseline disruption” is a proposed design tactic, not an established eye-tracking rule. Gratuitous shifts can also make scanning harder.
- **Task and motivation:** a person looking for a number, comparing options, or studying a method may attend to different regions of the same page. Name the task before choosing anchors or evaluating a design. A free-viewing saliency map cannot replace task-based observation.
