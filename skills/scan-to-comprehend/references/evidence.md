# Evidence and limits

S2C is a practical synthesis, not a validated scoring system or a predictive eye-tracking model.

- [Nielsen Norman Group, Text Scanning Patterns](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-scanning-patterns-eyetracking/) describes F-pattern, spotted, layer-cake, and commitment patterns. It stresses that task, assumptions, layout, and content affect scanning. Use the patterns to frame hypotheses, not to claim every reader follows one route.
- [Nielsen Norman Group, F-shaped Pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/) explains common misreadings of the F-pattern. It is not a bounce-rate formula or a fixed percentage of ignored content.
- [Nielsen Norman Group, Layer-Cake Pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/) supports meaningful headings as a way to help readers locate relevant content.
- [W3C, WCAG 2.2 Understanding 2.4.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/headings-and-labels) explains why headings and labels should describe topic or purpose. [Understanding 1.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/info-and-relationships) covers programmatic structure and relationships. An S2C review is not a WCAG conformance audit.
- [Chakraborty et al., Predicting Visual Attention in Graphic Design Documents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02439) models attention in free viewing of graphic designs. A model prediction is not proof that a particular layout improves comprehension.

Separate three kinds of statement in output:

1. **Observed:** a heading is absent, a metric has no unit, or a chart's legend is distant.
2. **Inferred:** this may make the reader's stated question harder to answer.
3. **Measured:** a defined reader task was tested with real participants or a suitable study and the result was recorded.

The optional structural script reports only the first category. Even a clean report says nothing about actual gaze, reading speed, comprehension, visual contrast, or mobile rendering.
