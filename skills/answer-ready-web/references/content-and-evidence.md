# Content and evidence

## Page brief

Establish these facts before drafting:

- audience and their current situation
- decision the page should enable
- offer and its practical outcome
- who should and should not use it
- next action and what happens afterward
- supplied proof, limitations, price, availability, and update owner

If a material fact is missing, use a visible placeholder in a draft or ask for it. Do not fill gaps with plausible copy.

## Opening answer

Near the H1, answer: what is it, who is it for, what outcome does it enable, and what constraint or differentiator matters? Prefer one compact paragraph. A quotable sentence is useful when it remains honest in isolation.

## Claim ledger

For each consequential claim, record:

| Claim | Type | Evidence reference | Scope | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exact sentence | measured, testimonial, credential, compatibility, price, or qualitative | URL, document, dataset, test, or named owner | population, version, date, and conditions | supported, qualified, missing, or remove |

Treat numbers, customer logos, rankings, superlatives, security statements, compatibility, and time savings as consequential. A link near a claim is not automatically adequate support; verify that it resolves and directly supports the statement.

## Decision-path structure

Select only sections the reader needs. A useful order is:

1. clear proposition and next action
2. problem and cost of the status quo
3. fit and exclusions
4. how the offer works
5. evidence and limitations
6. alternatives, pricing, or implementation details when relevant
7. real objections and next step

Question headings are helpful when readers actually ask the question. Specific declarative headings can be clearer. Avoid producing near-duplicate pages for query variants.

## Comparisons and FAQs

Compare stable, sourced dimensions and state the comparison date. Link to first-party sources for competitor facts where possible. Avoid unverifiable “winner” badges.

Use FAQs for unresolved decisions, operational questions, or objections. Keep answers visible in the page. FAQ structured data is not a default requirement and should not be presented as an AI-citation mechanism.
