Laws of UX Review

Annotated findings for a SaaS landing page

https://example.com/pricing

1

Hick's Law

Four equal hero CTAs compete for the first decision.

Keep one primary. Demote the rest to text links.

2

Fitts's Law

The submit control is small and far from the form fields.

Enlarge it and place it on the thumb arc, next to the last field.

3

Jakob's Law

Logo is not a home link. Nav labels invent new names for standard destinations.

Link the logo home. Use the category's usual labels.

4

Miller's Law

Nine peer feature items sit in one undifferentiated row.

Chunk to three groups. Reveal the rest on demand.

5

Proximity

Labels sit far from the inputs they name.

Pair each label with its field. Group related fields.

logo
6

Von Restorff

Every plan is emphasized, so none is remembered.

Emphasize one recommended plan. Leave the others quiet.

7

Doherty Threshold

Submit gives no acknowledgement inside a few hundred milliseconds.

Optimistic state or an inline pending status on the button.

8

Peak-End Rule

The flow ends on a blank thank-you with no next step.

Close on the outcome and one clear action.

9

Postel's Law

The phone field rejects common formats the user could not predict.

Accept messy input. Normalize it. Show the expected shape.

10

Serial position

The key destination is buried in the middle of the nav.

Place durable items first and last.
