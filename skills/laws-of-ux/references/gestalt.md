# Gestalt

If a layout fails several of these at once, file one grouping finding. Name the law that dictates the fix.

## Proximity

Items near each other are read as related (Wertheimer 1923). A label closer to the field below it than to its own field will be misread.

Flag: labels separated from inputs, related actions split across a region, a heading closer to the previous section.

Fix: tighten the pair, open space between groups.

## Similarity

Items that look alike are read as a group, even when spaced apart. Same color, size, and shape means "same kind of thing."

Flag: a destructive button styled like the primary; unrelated links sharing the exact treatment of the nav.

Fix: one treatment per role. Difference in role needs a difference in form.

## Uniform connectedness

An explicit connector — a line, arrow, or shared fill — groups items more strongly than proximity alone (Palmer and Rock 1994).

Flag: a step sequence with no visible thread; a chart legend that does not connect to its series.

Fix: connect the items that are one unit. Do not connect items that are not.

## Common region

A shared boundary groups items even when spacing is loose (Palmer 1992). Cards, wells, and panels do this.

Flag: a card that contains unrelated actions; related fields with no shared region and no proximity.

Fix: bound the group, or stop bounding things that are not a group.

## Prägnanz

People resolve an ambiguous figure into the simplest form that fits (Wertheimer). Sometimes spelled Pragnanz. One obvious reading.

Flag: an icon that needs a legend, a progress mark that can be read as a rating, a layout whose columns can be scanned either way.

Fix: reduce the figure until one reading remains. Do not cite Prägnanz for ordinary dense text; that is [decision principles](decision.md) Miller or Hick.
